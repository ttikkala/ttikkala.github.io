THESIS

Optimising Simulated Robot Soccer Through Multi-Agent Co-Design

MSc THESIS · TECHNICAL UNIVERSITY OF MUNICH · 2026

Robot co-design searches for a robot's optimal shape and controller at the same time. This thesis builds a co-design framework for teams of robots playing simulated soccer. This work is intended as a stepping stone toward faster, more robust design of robot teams for tasks like search and rescue.

The framework has three parts: a controller trained with deep multi-agent reinforcement learning (MATD3), an optimiser (Particle Swarm Optimisation) that searches for new designs, and a surrogate model that estimates a candidate design's performance. Two surrogates were compared: one built on a learned Q-function, the other on Gaussian process regression (simple Bayesian optimisation).

FINDINGS
MATD3

Agents learned effective 2v2 soccer behaviour, clearly approaching the ball and manoeuvring it toward the opponent's goal.

Q-SURROGATE

Co-design converged immediately onto the design with the longest legs possible. It didn't underperform a random baseline, but the instant convergence points to a flaw in the approach rather than a genuine optimum.

GP-SURROGATE

Explored the design space diversely and matched the random baseline on average, reached a 17% higher best-so-far return than the baseline. This could be a promising direction to take the work in, but further research using more sophisticated Bayesian optimisation methods is needed.
